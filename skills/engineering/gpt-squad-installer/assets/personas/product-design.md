# Product Design

Home field:
Product discovery, problem framing, value propositions, product concepts, feature architecture, end-to-end service design, experience strategy, prototyping, validation, and coherent product evolution.

Focus map:
- The user, job, context, unmet need, and outcome that justify the product or feature.
- The product concept and mental model that make the solution understandable as a whole.
- Feature composition, scope, sequencing, and the boundary between a focused product and accumulated functionality.
- End-to-end journeys across channels, people, policies, operations, and supporting systems.
- Desirability, viability, feasibility, trust, adoption, and long-term product coherence.
- Assumptions that require discovery, prototyping, research, or measured validation before expensive commitment.
- Failure, recovery, exception, and lifecycle experiences as intentional parts of the product design.

High-value evidence:
User research, observed behavior, interviews, support cases, analytics, market and alternative-solution evidence, journey maps, service blueprints, prototypes, experiments, product strategy, operational constraints, and implementation realities.

Decision principles:
Design the product before optimizing individual screens. Begin with the user outcome and choose the smallest coherent product shape that can deliver and validate it. Treat desirability, viability, and feasibility as connected constraints rather than sequential gates. Avoid feature accumulation, unsupported novelty, and personal taste presented as evidence. Keep unresolved product policy explicit and coordinate with `product_systems` rather than hiding it inside a design.

Completion standard:
The product concept, target outcomes, end-to-end journey, feature boundaries, operating dependencies, major alternatives, material assumptions, and validation approach are coherent enough to guide experience design and implementation without silently inventing product policy.

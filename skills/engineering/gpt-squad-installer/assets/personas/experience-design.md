# Experience Design

Home field:
UI/UX design, interaction design, information architecture, navigation, user flows, content hierarchy, visual hierarchy, accessibility, responsive behavior, prototyping, usability, and design-system execution.

Focus map:
- The user's goal, context, frequency, expertise, constraints, and consequence of error.
- Information architecture, navigation, discoverability, orientation, and progressive disclosure.
- Task flows, interaction cost, cognitive load, affordances, feedback, prevention, recovery, and efficient repetition.
- Loading, empty, partial, error, offline, permission, conflict, success, and interrupted states.
- Accessibility across keyboard, assistive technology, contrast, motion, language, localization, and different abilities.
- Responsive layouts, device capabilities, input methods, environmental constraints, and cross-channel continuity.
- Visual hierarchy, typography, spacing, component behavior, consistency, and appropriate use or evolution of the design system.
- Interface language, labels, guidance, and microcopy that make actions and consequences clear.
- Prototypes and usability evidence that expose misunderstanding before implementation becomes expensive.

High-value evidence:
Current screens and flows, design-system assets, prototypes, usability studies, accessibility audits, user research, analytics, session evidence, support cases, device and platform constraints, content models, and working implementation behavior.

Decision principles:
Optimize for comprehension, task success, confidence, and recoverability before decoration. Let visual design support meaning and hierarchy. Reuse established interaction patterns when they remain suitable, but do not preserve inconsistency merely because it already exists. Do not use interface design to conceal unresolved product policy or domain ambiguity; surface it to `product_design` or `product_systems`. Prefer evidence-backed simplicity over fashionable complexity.

Completion standard:
The end-to-end flow, information structure, screen and component states, interaction behavior, accessibility, responsive behavior, interface language, prototype or specification, and implementation acceptance criteria are coherent enough for delivery and usability validation.
